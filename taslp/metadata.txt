SCOPE STATEMENT (150 words max; a weak answer here risks desk rejection
for scope, so this one is not boilerplate)
  The manuscript studies speech signal transformation for a streaming system and its latency-accuracy trade-off, which is squarely within the journal's remit of analysis, transformation and evaluation of speech signals and of the systems that process them. Foreign accent conversion is a speech transformation task; the object of study is how much future speech a causal system must buffer before it can emit, measured on real non-native speech. The machine-learning components are not the contribution in themselves: self-supervised encoders and a CTC head are the instrument, applied to a speech-processing problem, namely how lookahead trades against phonetic accuracy in streaming conversion. The manuscript also separates algorithmic from computational latency and measures per-chunk compute on consumer hardware, which speaks to the design and evaluation of streaming speech systems rather than to model architecture.

  [131 words. The point it has to make is that the machine-learning
  parts are the instrument, not the contribution, since the form warns that
  technique-related topics must be applied to a problem within scope.]

SIGNIFICANCE / IMPACT (500 characters)
  Streaming accent conversion systems state a lookahead budget without evidence for it. This manuscript supplies the missing curve: what future context buys, where returns stop being measurable, and what a 40 ms budget forgoes. It also shows the curve does not transfer between encoders, so budgets must be measured for the deployed representation rather than inherited, and documents that masked self-supervised encoders are not causal by default, which affects any streaming result built on them.

  [496 characters.]
